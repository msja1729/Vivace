An utterance is one stretch of speech between pauses: a sentence, or a few sentences said without stopping. The app listens for those pauses and sends each stretch to Deepgram as a separate small clip. This was already part of the app's design; switching to Deepgram kept it. Example Say the student answers: > "I used PostgreSQL… *(pause)* … because the data was relational." The app hears this: 
[ speech: "I used PostgreSQL" ] [pause 1 s] [ speech: "because the data was relational" ] [silence…]
        ▲ utterance 1                              ▲ utterance 2
Each utterance becomes one clip, which Deepgram turns into one line in the transcript. How the app finds them A small voice detector checks the audio every 30 milliseconds: speech or not? - Start: when it hears speech clearly. It also keeps the 0.15 s just before, so the first word isn't cut off. - End: after 0.75 s of silence. - Long talk: if someone talks for more than 20 s without pausing, it's cut there so the transcript still updates. Why do it this way 1. No silence is sent. Deepgram charges per second of audio, and in a viva much of the time nobody speaks. Sending only speech saves credit and bandwidth. 2. The transcript appears live. Each utterance shows up a moment after it's spoken, instead of waiting for the end of the viva. 3. Answers can be graded as they come. The AI groups the student's utterances after your question into "their answer", grades it, and the Co-Pilot suggests follow-ups. 4. The one downside If someone pauses for more than about 0.75 s in the middle of a sentence, the sentence is split into two lines. The meaning is all still there and the AI grades the whole answer together, but the transcript can look a bit choppy. If that bothers you, there's an alternative: Deepgram's live streaming mode.
